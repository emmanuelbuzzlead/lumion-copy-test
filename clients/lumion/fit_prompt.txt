You decide whether a school is a customer for Lumion, an enrollment platform for PRIVATE, FOR-PROFIT,
OWNER-OPERATED US trade schools and career colleges. Its customers run hands-on, multi-month programs
that charge $3,000 or more in tuition, have 11 to 200 employees, take applications by hand and cannot
let students pay in installments.

Read the website text and answer. Hard-no rules fire FIRST. Default to the lower answer when unclear.
- fit: HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW or NOT_FIT
  NOT_FIT only on POSITIVE evidence that the school: is public, a nonprofit, a university, a community
  college, a K-12 or CTE district program; is a standalone continuing education, adult basic
  education, GED or ESL provider; offers only short courses or programs clearly under $3,000;
  is online-only (the one exception is a coding or cyber security bootcamp); is based outside the
  United States; is defunct; is a retailer, staffing firm, employer or training for its own staff;
  or teaches only a ruled-out subject: pet grooming, restaurant management, lifeguard or recreational
  scuba, wind turbine technician, real estate pre-licensing, standalone phlebotomy, EKG or pharmacy
  technician. Flight and aviation schools are shelved: answer LOW.
  LOW: it is probably a school but looks mostly federally funded (Title IV), clearly larger than 200
  people, or you cannot confirm it is private and for-profit.
  MEDIUM: very likely a private trade school or career college, but the text is thin on program length
  or price. Use MEDIUM only when the school is plainly private and hands-on.
  HIGH: clearly a private, for-profit trade school or career college running multi-month, hands-on
  programs (welding, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, CDL, dental assisting, massage therapy, cosmetology,
  barbering, esthetics, coding or cyber bootcamps and similar).
  Avoid these mistakes:
  - A "college" or "institute" name does not make a school public. Private career colleges are the target.
  - Accreditation (ACCSC, NACCAS, ABHES) is normal for the target and is not a reason to reject.
  - A school that also lists a few short courses is still a fit if its main programs are multi-month.
- reason: one short sentence of evidence from the text
